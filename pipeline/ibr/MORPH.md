## What the footage is doing

Use the unmodified viewer chase-track for the host scene. Its position and orientation use the same reconstruction frame as the stitched camera trajectory: on six probes between 85 and 140 s the two ego positions agreed within 1 mm. Convert the Three camera basis to sensor convention with diag(1,−1,−1). The supplied clips are 1440×900, 30 fps, vfov 50°. Both endpoints are physical frames; the 110–125 s interval thus has 451 frames. Nothing in the movie draws the ego, ribbon, HUD or tracked vehicle models.

1. Project the chase eye onto nearby path segments in the **full** front-camera frame table. Smooth arc length over two output frames and enforce forward progress; bracket that position with consecutive sensor exposures. At slow speed the delayed clock advances slowly or holds. On the exit, score historical front and pillar headings as well as distance, and ease in the lens that actually faces the turn. The clip uses multiple distance-back brackets for front coverage before pillar fill. Extra orientation matches can switch; this is one remaining source of motion artifacts.
2. Propagate the two adjacent depth keyframes through their physical camera transforms onto each video exposure, then interpolate **inverse axial depth**. Dynamic masks are dilated and propagated with them. Keep the road on its measured tangent plane; smooth distant inverse depth bilaterally and extend it behind near relief. Use the observed ground contact where consistent with depth. A near/static matte gates the second relief surface. Sky tends to zero inverse depth; the private dawn texture fills gaps. Photographic texture coordinates are continuous, without cut meshes or object cards. These are approximate relief depths: the back of a tree or sign is not measured by a single frontal view.
3. For each target ray, backward-project into both source sheets. Bound the differential departure from a smooth reference chart to avoid per-pixel folding. Reject high-anisotropy road samples where the measured stitched ground is available. Draw neither delayed instances nor artificial ego geometry. Fill uncovered samples from other warped footage before using the host ground and sky; the small remaining inter-lens gaps use spatial continuation, not real newly observed surface.
4. In chase space, estimate dense luma flow on a 288×180 grid over valid overlap (Farnebäck, range-limited, smoothed). If A→B displacement is v and bracket fraction is f, sample A at x−fv and B at x+(1−f)v. Blend only after those warps. For the flanks, register the supplementary image to the accumulated higher-resolution image; move the donor, keep the centre fixed. Upsample the resulting coordinate maps and sample the original corrected textures. This preserves more detail than simply resizing a coarse render.

## Runtime input for 110–125 s

- `bank.json` indexes six contiguous sensor runs and the per-chase-frame ordered list of (lens, lower exposure number, fractional weight). Acquisition timestamps, camera-to-world matrices, intrinsics and native calibration dimensions accompany the records. Each chase sample also stores the tangent ground-plane equation and target CV pose; the archive includes its infinity sky texture. RGB is already corrected with the stitched exposure/shading model. Each source run is a silent 36 fps H.264 texture video; use acquisition timestamps, not MP4 wall time, for pair selection. Exposure n corresponds to its record index in the run. Adjacent exposure n+1 is the second member.
- Every source frame has a 148×96 grid, 8 bytes/texel: little-endian u16 background and near inverse-z (divide by 65,535, in m⁻¹), then u8 near alpha, dynamic exclusion, ground alpha and valid alpha. Read compressed frames by offset/length. Every 16th is absolute; intermediate frames are zlib-compressed byte differences modulo 256 from the preceding frame. Full delta-chain readback and texture frame counts were checked.
- **1,882 source frames; 72.29 MB textures, 55.66 MB geometry, 0.96 MB metadata plus a 39 kB sky.** Archive is 128.15 MB, or **8.54 MB per second of chase playback** if fetched once. Geometry alone is 3.71 MB/s. This is a cold asset budget, not a GPU memory, bandwidth-at-steady-state or performance benchmark. Decode ahead; retain the two textures/grids from each contributing run, not the entire archive on the GPU.
- A live implementation uploads the two depth channels and mattes as textures, recomputes the ray lookup from the orbit eye, does a low-resolution alignment pass, and resolves full-resolution photo colour over the host ground. The provided default-chase selection schedule is a starting point for small orbits. Re-evaluate overlapping lens coverage after rotation. Residual flow is **not** stored: it depends on the target pose. No performance or orbit-fidelity claim is made for the existing viewer; it plays the MP4s.

## Motion verdict and measurements

The 110–125 s road is photographic in the middle; **the whole frame does not read as one continuous moving photograph**. From the [published viewer recording](report-m/110-125/viewer.mp4), look at the median beside the car and the tree/kerb on the right. The tree changes silhouette and stripes from the older flank donor bend differently from those on the road. At the exit, the heading search brings the left pillar forward around 100–101 s and the right around 109 s. It preserves coverage but does not register the nearer structures well enough. Real footage has a cool tint; some fallback trees remain lavender. No ego or tracked vehicle models are rendered into the movie. Delayed photograph masks are only as complete as the source detections; there is no all-frame guarantee that they removed every vehicle.

- [Front footage / chase / splats in motion](report-m/110-125/compare.mp4). The left camera is the physical, same-drive-time front camera; the middle and right use the exact chase eye. For sharpness, instead compare the centre against **its actual delayed source**, not the different-eye left panel. Take P90 Sobel luma magnitude within the middle 40% of the image, excluding foreground and weakly supported samples; then take the median across whole-second probes. 110–125 s: source 3.93, source warped to chase scale 3.52, first road clip 3.30, [published 55 s encode](report-m/85-140/road-sharpness.json) 3.07 and splats 2.54. The final encode retains 78% of native source edge strength in that region and exceeds the splats by 21%. Gradients depend on contrast and scale; this isn't an MTF measurement.
- [Stretch map](report-m/110-125/stretch.jpg) · [donor attribution](report-m/110-125/donor-audit.json). For each significant background contributor (weight >0.35, near alpha <0.30), estimate singular values of the output→source mapping. Inverse smallest singular value is enlargement in the worst direction. More than 6× enlargement covers 2.0%, 3.9%, 4.3% and 8.9% of the whole image at 110, 112, 117.5 and 125 s. The largest connected >6× patch at 125 s occupies 72,550 pixels; its median enlargement is 14.25×. Front exposure 4419 (123.297 s) accounts for more stretched samples than any pillar. This lower bound excludes silhouettes and blind-angle extension. The frame-median of P95 anisotropy, a *different* measure, is 10.35:1.
- [Donor audit](report-m/110-125/donor-audit.json): where the primary background would see a masked near object but its foreground sheet leaves a hole, independent donors supplied 48–88% of colour weight across the four probes. In the three probes with primary dynamic masks, other photographs supplied 65–99%. Neither coverage figure proves the replacement is the correct world surface.
- The two media files at each join originally used separately smoothed trajectory and camera-selection clocks. Match both endpoint schedules over 1.1 s; then pack the four intervals as one 85–140 s video, dropping three repeated endpoints. This avoids the existing player's 0.3 s fade to splats at a media change. Separate interval assets remain available for diagnosis.

## After-image release gate

**The existing published preview is a failing control, not an accepted M render. The single-owner replacement was rejected and remains unpublished.** A candidate is staged privately until its encoded SHA-256 has an all-frame audit. Sharpness and photographic coverage cannot pass this gate.

The staged resolver aligns adjacent temporal brackets in chase space, then takes one actual exposure per region. Foreground and background coordinates, supplementary front/pillar photographs, and the stitched underlay are separate categorical choices. Graph-cut penalties increase near strong gradients and cross-source disagreement; previous ownership is advected into each frame. A second contour pass tries to keep conflicting silhouettes on one photograph when its validity and mover masks permit. RGB is never feathered between different owners. This alone cannot prevent a seam from showing both sides of a displaced pole.

**Audit:** decode the H.264 clips, run Canny on luma, and sample every fourth strong edge pixel distributed across the full frame. Look in both normal directions for a same-facing, nearly parallel second edge 3–40 pixels away. Count it if neither endpoint's standalone warp contains both edges within a one-pixel neighbourhood and adjacent anchors support the pair along its tangent. Count each sampled anchor at most once. A separate upper-frame band covers the skyline and building/pole areas; it is not semantic segmentation. Capture explicit office roof, utility pole, streetlamp, signal arm and sign ROIs separately. The published blend is compared against its dominant warp; where there is no photographic owner the reference is the ground/sky underlay. An individually encoded single-source warp estimates the compression/detector floor, and a deliberately shifted photograph tests detector sensitivity. This detector misses faint edges, separations outside 3–40 pixels and differently oriented contours; passing it would be necessary, not proof of universal absence.
